BLOG 1

Title:

AML Training That People Actually Remember

Header Image

Generate an editorial-style image of a small professional compliance training session with participants actively discussing a scenario around a table. Avoid generic auditorium/classroom imagery. Professional UK office environment, restrained vermilion accents, no text embedded in image.

Article

AML training can easily become an annual exercise in completion rather than learning.

Slides are presented. Regulations are summarised. Attendance is recorded.

Then everybody returns to work.

Effective training should instead help employees recognise situations in which AML risk appears in their actual role.

Start with scenarios.

Ask participants what they would do when information does not make sense. Introduce incomplete facts. Allow different interpretations. Ask what additional information they would request and when they would escalate.

The objective is not simply to teach employees what the policy says.

It is to help them recognise when the policy becomes relevant.

Training should therefore combine:

short explanations

realistic scenarios

audience participation

decision points

escalation exercises

examples relevant to the organisation's clients and matters

The strongest AML training often leaves participants with better questions rather than longer notes.

CompliancePlus Perspective

If employees understand why a control exists, they are considerably more likely to apply it intelligently.

BLOG 2

Title:

What SRA AML Reviews Can Teach Law Firms About Their Own Controls

Header Image

Generate a restrained editorial image of a legal/compliance professional reviewing an audit file with structured documents and highlighted risk observations. UK law-firm setting, vermilion accent details, no logos or embedded text.

Article

AML supervision should not be viewed purely as an exercise in demonstrating that policies exist.

The more useful question is whether those policies operate effectively when applied to real clients and real matters.

Regulatory reviews of law firms repeatedly highlight the importance of practical implementation across areas such as:

client and matter risk assessment

source of funds and source of wealth

ongoing monitoring

documentation of decisions

appropriate escalation

consistency between written procedures and actual practice

A technically comprehensive AML policy therefore provides limited comfort if the operational process underneath it does not consistently produce good decisions.

Firms should periodically test their own files from the perspective of an external reviewer.

Ask:

Could another experienced professional understand why this client was accepted?

Could they identify the risks considered?

Could they see what mitigated those risks?

Could they understand who approved the decision and why?

If the answer is unclear, the weakness may not be the underlying decision.

It may be the process used to evidence it.

CompliancePlus Perspective

An AML framework should be designed not merely to survive regulatory inspection, but to produce consistently defensible decisions before an inspection ever occurs.

IMPORTANT: Before publishing this article, verify every statement referring to SRA findings against current SRA publications and add source links. Do not fabricate statistics, findings or regulatory statements.

BLOG 3

Title:

The FCA Is Your Friend — Not Your Foe

Header Image

Generate a sophisticated editorial image representing constructive dialogue between regulation and business: two professionals reviewing a compliance framework together across a table. No FCA logo. Restrained red/black/white palette. No embedded text.

Article

Regulators are sometimes discussed inside organisations as though their objective were to catch businesses making mistakes.

That mindset can produce defensive compliance.

A healthier approach is to treat regulatory expectations as information about the standards the organisation should be capable of demonstrating.

When the FCA publishes guidance, thematic observations, enforcement outcomes or supervisory findings, compliance teams should ask:

What does this tell us about the regulator's expectations of firms like ours?

Then translate that insight into operational questions.

Are our procedures clear?

Do employees understand them?

Does our management information identify emerging problems?

Are exceptions visible?

Can senior management understand where residual risks remain?

Would our records allow us to explain a decision several years later?

Regulatory publications can therefore become a free source of control-design intelligence.

CompliancePlus Perspective

Strong compliance functions should not spend their time fearing the regulator.

They should build organisations capable of explaining confidently how risks are identified, managed and governed.

IMPORTANT: Do not imply FCA endorsement of CompliancePlus. Verify regulatory statements and link directly to relevant current FCA materials before publication.

BLOG 4

Title:

Matter Risk Assessment: The Other Half of the AML Picture

Header Image

Generate an editorial visual showing one client connected to several different legal matters represented by separate structured folders or pathways, illustrating different risk profiles. Professional, minimal, vermilion accent, no text.

Article

A client's identity does not tell you everything about the risk created by a particular instruction.

The same client can instruct a firm on two matters with very different AML characteristics.

Consider the difference between a routine advisory instruction and a transaction involving:

unusual payment arrangements

third-party funding

high-risk jurisdictions

complex corporate structures

significant movement of funds

unexpected changes in transaction behaviour

The client may not have changed.

The risk presented by the matter may have.

That is why client risk assessment and matter risk assessment should work together.

A useful matter risk assessment should encourage the fee earner and AML team to consider what is actually happening, why it makes commercial sense and whether the proposed activity changes the firm's exposure.

CompliancePlus Perspective

Know your client.

But also understand what your client is asking you to do.

IMPORTANT: Align the final published article with current UK legal-sector AML requirements and guidance before publication.

BLOG 5

Title:

Your Client Risk Assessment Form Should Evolve With Your Business

Header Image

Generate a clean editorial image of a compliance professional redesigning a structured digital risk-assessment form, with branching decision pathways represented visually. Vermilion accents, no readable confidential information, no text overlay.

Article

A Client Risk Assessment form should not become permanent simply because it once worked.

Businesses change.

Client populations change.

Products change.

Geographic exposure changes.

Regulatory expectations evolve.

And compliance teams learn from the cases that pass through them.

Your CRA should therefore be treated as an operational control that requires periodic refinement.

Review questions such as:

Are analysts repeatedly requesting information that the onboarding form could have collected earlier?

Are certain questions producing ambiguous answers?

Are risk factors being captured but not meaningfully affecting the assessment?

Are senior approvers receiving the information they actually need?

Are employees writing long narratives because the form itself is poorly structured?

The best risk-assessment forms guide thought without replacing judgment.

CompliancePlus Perspective

Do not ask whether your CRA form is complete.

Ask whether it consistently helps people make better risk decisions.

BLOG 6

Title:

Source of Wealth: Ask the Story Before Asking for the Documents

Header Image

Generate an elegant editorial image showing a chronological financial journey represented through several legitimate wealth-building stages — career, business ownership, investment and asset sale — flowing into a structured compliance review. Professional, restrained, no currency spectacle, no text.

Article

Source of wealth reviews can become unnecessarily inefficient when the process begins by requesting documents before understanding the underlying story.

A better starting point is often the narrative.

How was the client's wealth accumulated?

Over what period?

Through employment?

Business ownership?

Investment?

Inheritance?

Asset disposal?

Multiple sources?

Once that story is understood, the compliance professional can determine what information or evidence is relevant.

An intelligent source-of-wealth questionnaire should therefore adapt to the client's answers.

For example, selecting business ownership should trigger questions about:

business activity

ownership percentage

period of ownership

approximate value

distributions or sale proceeds

relevant supporting information

An inheritance pathway requires an entirely different set of questions.

The objective is not to create the longest possible questionnaire.

It is to collect enough structured information in the first interaction to understand the wealth narrative and identify what needs verification.

CompliancePlus Perspective

Understand the story first.

Then decide what evidence the story requires.

IMPORTANT: Do not present this article as definitive regulatory guidance. Ensure final publication reflects applicable regulatory requirements and the organisation's own risk-based procedures.
